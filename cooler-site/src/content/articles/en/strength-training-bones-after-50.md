## What happens

Estrogen helps keep calcium in your bones. When it drops, bone breaks down faster than it is rebuilt. According to the health department of Victoria, Australia, women lose up to 10% of their bone density on average in the first five years after menopause.

Muscle mass goes down at the same time. That makes falls more likely, and with fragile bones a fall is more likely to end in a fracture.

## What kind of exercise

- Strength training two or three times a week: sit-to-stand from a chair, wall push-ups, exercises with dumbbells or a resistance band.
- Weight-bearing exercise: brisk walking, stairs, dancing.
- Balance work: stand on one leg while holding the back of a chair.
- Start small and build up slowly. If you already have osteoporosis, past fractures or strong pain, see your doctor or a physical therapist first.

## Food

Calcium is in dairy, firm tofu, sardines with bones, almonds and leafy greens. Vitamin D helps your body absorb it. Protein at every meal helps you keep muscle. Alcohol and smoking speed up bone loss.

## Which checks to get

A bone density scan (DXA) measures how dense your bones are. The US Preventive Services Task Force recommends it for all women from 65, and earlier for women at higher risk: early menopause, a parent who broke a hip, low body weight, smoking, or long-term use of steroid medicines.
