## Why sleep falls apart

After menopause, several things get in the way at once. Night sweats wake you up. Worries keep you from falling back asleep. You need the bathroom more often. Your joints ache. And sleep apnea, where breathing briefly stops during sleep, becomes more common after menopause.

So it helps to figure out what exactly is waking you. Logging sleep and hot flashes in the app will show you.

## Insomnia: what works

If poor sleep has lasted more than three months, doctors recommend cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) first. It is done with a specialist, and there are online programs too. Here are a few of its basic rules you can start with now:

- Get up at the same time every day, even after a bad night and on weekends.
- Go to bed only when you actually feel sleepy, not because it is “time”.
- If you cannot sleep for about twenty minutes, get up and do something calm in dim light. Go back to bed when you feel sleepy.
- Keep the bed for sleep and sex. No phone, TV or work.

## Snoring and sleep apnea

If you snore, your partner notices pauses in your breathing, and you feel wiped out during the day even after eight hours in bed, tell your doctor. Sleep apnea can be tested for and treated, and it matters for your heart, not just your sleep.

## If hot flashes wake you

A cool bedroom, a light blanket and cotton bedding make sleep more comfortable. But if hot flashes wake you most nights, that is a strong reason to talk to your doctor about treating the hot flashes themselves.
