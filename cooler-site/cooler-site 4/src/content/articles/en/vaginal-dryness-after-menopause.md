## What happens

Without estrogen, the tissues of the vagina and urethra get thinner and drier, there is less natural lubrication, and the vaginal flora changes. That leads to burning, itching, painful sex, needing to pee more often, and bladder infections. Depending on the study, half or more of women after menopause have this.

## What you can do yourself

- Use a vaginal moisturizer regularly, two or three times a week, not only before sex.
- During sex, use a water-based or silicone-based lubricant.
- Wash only the outside with warm water or a gentle unscented cleanser. Douching is not needed.
- Regular sexual activity, including on your own, supports blood flow and keeps tissues more elastic.

## When to see a doctor

If moisturizers are not enough, your doctor may suggest local low-dose estrogen. It works mainly where it is applied. There are prescription non-hormonal options too. If there is pain and muscle tension, a pelvic floor physical therapist can help.

## Sex after menopause

Desire changes for many women after menopause, and it does not mean something is wrong with you. You do not have to put up with pain: it is a signal, and it can be treated. It helps to talk with your partner about what has changed, and not to rush.
